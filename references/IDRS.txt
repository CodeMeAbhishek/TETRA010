To integrate the **Madras Diabetes Research Foundation-Indian Diabetes Risk Score (MDRF-IDRS)** into your Rule-Based Clinical Scoring Engine, here is the in-depth extraction of the algorithmic rules, risk thresholds, and clinical logic based on the ICMR-INDIAB population-based study. 

This score is specifically designed as a simple, fast, and non-invasive tool to identify individuals at high risk for undiagnosed type 2 diabetes in the Asian Indian population, allowing for targeted blood glucose screening.

### 1. The IDRS Scoring Algorithm (For your Rule-Based Engine)
The MDRF-IDRS calculates risk using four simple parameters with a maximum possible score of 100. Your backend should calculate the sum of the following variables:

**A. Age (Years)**
*   **< 35 years:** 0 points
*   **35 to 49 years:** 20 points
*   **$\ge$ 50 years:** 30 points

**B. Waist Circumference** *(Note: Cutoffs differ by sex due to the Asian Indian phenotype of greater abdominal adiposity)*
*   **Female < 80 cm** OR **Male < 90 cm:** 0 points
*   **Female 80–89 cm** OR **Male 90–99 cm:** 10 points
*   **Female $\ge$ 90 cm** OR **Male $\ge$ 100 cm:** 20 points

**C. Physical Activity**
*   **Vigorous:** Regular vigorous exercise or strenuous (manual) work at home/work: 0 points
*   **Moderate:** Regular moderate exercise or moderate physical activity at home/work: 10 points
*   **Mild:** Regular mild exercise or mild physical activity at home/work: 20 points
*   **Sedentary:** No exercise and sedentary activities at home/work: 30 points

**D. Family History of Diabetes**
*   **No diabetes in parents:** 0 points
*   **One parent is diabetic:** 10 points
*   **Both parents are diabetic:** 20 points

### 2. Risk Stratification Thresholds
Once the engine sums the points, it should classify the patient into one of three risk categories:
*   **Low Risk:** Score **< 30**
*   **Moderate Risk:** Score **30 to 50**
*   **High Risk:** Score **$\ge$ 60**

### 3. Missing-Investigation Recommender Logic
The purpose of the IDRS is to act as a preliminary, cost-effective screening step to avoid "universal screening". 
*   **Rule:** If a patient is categorized as **Moderate Risk (30-50)** or **High Risk ($\ge$60)**, your Missing-Investigation Recommender should prompt the clinician to order a definitive glucose test. 
*   **Tests to recommend:** The study validates the score against **Fasting Capillary Blood Glucose** and the **Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT)**. 

### 4. Engine Validation & Context (For the LLM Orchestration Layer)
If your LLM orchestration layer needs to explain the *"why"* behind an IDRS score or justify a referral decision to a clinician or patient, it can cite these study statistics:
*   **High Capture Rate:** In the massive ICMR-INDIAB national survey (over 113,000 subjects), the MDRF-IDRS successfully categorized **96.1% of all newly diagnosed diabetic patients** into either the moderate or high-risk categories (60.2% in high-risk, 35.9% in moderate-risk). 
*   **Pre-diabetes Capture:** The score also successfully identified 91.3% of individuals with *pre-diabetes* as being in the moderate or high-risk categories. 
*   **Demographic Insights:** Individuals who score highly on the IDRS generally present with higher systolic and diastolic blood pressure, central obesity (waist circumference), and are older. Females were also found to be more likely to fall into the high-risk score category than males across both urban and rural settings. 
*   **Accuracy:** Overall, the scoring tool has a sensitivity of roughly 60.2%, a specificity of 68.8%, and an accuracy of 68.5% in identifying undiagnosed diabetes.

*Architectural Note:* Because the IDRS heavily relies on waist circumference (which is a better metric for the Indian population than BMI), your Input Validator must ensure that **waist circumference is a strictly mandatory field** for the IDRS module to run accurately.



Here are the mathematical formulas and calculations explicitly detailed in the provided sources:

### 1. Hemodynamic Formulas (ACC/AHA Hypertension Guidelines)
Table 4 of the guidelines defines several derived blood pressure components used in epidemiological evaluations:
*   **Pulse Pressure:** $\text{Systolic Blood Pressure (SBP)} - \text{Diastolic Blood Pressure (DBP)}$.
*   **Mean Arterial Pressure:** $\text{DBP} + \frac{\text{Pulse Pressure}}{3}$ (assuming a normal heart rate).
*   **Mid-BP:** $\frac{\text{SBP} + \text{DBP}}{2}$.

*(Note: While the guidelines heavily rely on the **ACC/AHA Pooled Cohort Equations** to calculate the 10-year ASCVD risk score for treatment thresholds, the underlying mathematical formulas for this equation are not printed in the text and are instead linked to an online calculator)*.

### 2. The MDRF-IDRS Scoring Formula (Indian Diabetes Risk Score)
The clinical formula for calculating a patient's diabetes risk is a simple summation of four assigned scores:
*   **Total IDRS Score (Maximum 100)** = $\text{Age Score} + \text{Waist Circumference Score} + \text{Physical Activity Score} + \text{Family History Score}$.
    *   *Age:* <35 years (0), 35-49 years (20), $\ge$50 years (30).
    *   *Waist:* Normal (0), Elevated (10), High (20) based on sex-specific cutoffs.
    *   *Activity:* Vigorous (0), Moderate (10), Mild (20), Sedentary (30).
    *   *Family History:* None (0), One parent (10), Both parents (20).

### 3. Epidemiological & Statistical Formulas (ICMR-INDIAB Study)
The appendix of the diabetes risk score study provides the specific statistical formulas used to conduct their nationwide population survey:

**Sample Size Calculation Formula:**
To determine how many people needed to be surveyed, the researchers used the following formula:
*   $n = \frac{Z^2 \times p \times q}{d^2}$
    *   *$Z$* = Z statistic for a 95% level of confidence (1.96).
    *   *$p$* = Estimated prevalence of diabetes.
    *   *$q$* = $(1 - p)$.
    *   *$d$* = Margin of error (set to 20% of $p$).
*   **Accounting for non-responders (20%):** $\text{Total Sample Size} = n + \frac{20 \times n}{100}$.

**Population Weighting Formulas:**
To ensure the survey data accurately represented the entire Indian population, the following weighting formulas were applied:
*   **Base Weight (BW):** $\frac{\text{Probability of selection}}{\text{Probability of selection in the domain}}$.
*   **Response Rate (RR):** $\frac{\text{Total number of responders}}{\text{Total sample size}}$.
*   **Adjusted Base Weight (ABW):** $\frac{\text{BW}}{\text{RR}}$.
*   **Normalized Weight (NW):** $ABW \times \text{Number of responders}$.
*   **Final Weight (for Urban or Rural domains):** $ABW \times \frac{\text{Total number of responders}}{\text{Overall NW}}$.
